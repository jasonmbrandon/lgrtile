Floor work

Everything that happens before the first tile.

Whole-house porcelain jobs are the volume of our work. The visible difference between a good floor and a great one is almost always prep.

Subfloor prep

Grinding high spots, filling low ones, checking for movement. A flat floor is what stops lippage at the joints.

Layout & pattern

Grid measured from the room’s sightlines, not the nearest wall. Off-square rooms get a layout that hides it.

Large-format tile

Bigger pieces need full back-buttering and a flat bed. Fewer grout lines, and no room for short cuts.

Transitions

Thresholds and tile-to-wood or tile-to-carpet edges set to the right height so nothing trips you.

Removal & haul-away

Old flooring out and taken away, so the room is ready for the new floor instead of stacked with the old one.

Baseboards & trim

Skirting refitted after the floor, or tiled skirting if you prefer it — decided before the layout, not after.

Natural stone & mosaic

Stone is slower. That is the point.

“Working with natural stone isn’t easy, but Lucas took his time and made sure every piece was placed perfectly.” That is a customer from July 2026, and it is how this work has to go.

  • Marble, travertine, slate and limestone, laid dry before they are bonded.
  • Shade and vein direction checked across every box before setting.
  • Mosaic sheets lined up so the joints read as one continuous field.
  • Stone sealed where the material calls for it, with the right product.

Natural stone varies from piece to piece. We will show you the layout dry before anything is fixed so there are no surprises about the pattern.

Questions

Floor questions, answered.

Can you lay tile over my existing floor?

Sometimes — over a sound, flat slab or a well-bonded tile floor. If the existing surface is not flat or not stable, the new tile will fail no matter how good the setting is, so we check the floor first and tell you what it needs.

Do you remove the old tile?

Yes. Demolition and haul-away are part of most floor jobs, and they are quoted as their own line so you can see what it costs.

How do you stop the floor feeling hollow or uneven?

Prep. High spots come down, low spots get filled, and the tile is set with full coverage rather than spotted at the corners. It is the least visible part of the job and the one you notice with bare feet.

Will the new floor line up with the rest of the house?

We set the height so thresholds and transitions sit flush, and we can match existing tile where the new floor meets it — the same job that got one customer’s kitchen to line up exactly.

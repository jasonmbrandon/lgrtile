What’s included

Everything behind the tile as well as on it.

Customers mostly review the tile. The reason a shower still looks right five years on is the work that went on two days before it.

  • A pre-sloped pan, built or set true, so water leaves instead of sitting.
  • Waterproofing over the substrate on walls and floor before tile goes up.
  • Niches, benches and corner shelves framed in properly, not stuck on.
  • Layout drawn out first, so cuts land where they look deliberate.
  • A floor tile with grip, not the same polished piece used on the walls.

The job, step by step

How a bathroom gets redone.

Same order every time. It is why the finished room has no surprises in it.

1. Strip and inspect

Old tile, backer and pan come out. What is underneath decides the next step — framing, subfloor, or a leak that never showed on the surface.

2. Waterproof

Substrate, then a continuous waterproof membrane across floor and walls, tied into the drain and up the walls behind the tile.

3. Set out the layout

Full tiles centred where you look first, cuts pushed to the corners, and grout lines that run straight from the floor to the ceiling.

4. Tile the room

Walls, floor, niche, bench and curb. Stone and mosaic take longer — each piece is checked before it is set.

5. Grout and seal

Grout lines cleaned and finished evenly, silicone at the changes of plane, stone sealed where it needs it.

6. Clean and walk through

The room is left clean and the work is walked with you before we pack up. Anything that needs a second look gets one.

Bathroom remodels

Floors, walls, tub areas — or the whole room.

Plenty of customers do not want a new bathroom. They want the pink tile gone, or a floor that is not slippery, or a tub surround that stops looking tired.

  • Bathroom floors in porcelain or natural stone, level and grouted clean.
  • Tub surrounds and wall tile, including the awkward cuts around fixtures.
  • Half-renovations: keep the tub, retile everything you can see.
  • Matching new tile to an old floor elsewhere in the house.

We are a tile crew, not a general contractor. If a job needs plumbing moved, framing or a new vanity fitted, we will tell you before you book.

Questions

What customers ask before booking.

Do you waterproof behind the tile?

Yes. Waterproofing goes on after the old shower comes out and before the first tile is set. It is the part you never see, and the reason a shower lasts.

Do I buy the tile or do you?

Either. Most customers buy their own tile and have it delivered; we will tell you how much to order once the job is measured. If you would rather pick from a showroom together, ask on the call.

Can you replace just the shower pan?

Sometimes, but not always. A pan is tied into the walls and the drain, so we look at how the whole enclosure is built first. We will tell you honestly whether a pan-only repair will hold or whether the shower needs to come out.

How long does a shower take?

It depends on the size, the substrate, the tile and how many details are going in. You get a dated window in your written quote before anything starts, and an update if it moves.

Will you work with the bathroom I already have?

Yes. Most of our work is a straight swap: same footprint, new tile. Moving a drain or a wall is a different job, and we will say so up front if it needs another trade.

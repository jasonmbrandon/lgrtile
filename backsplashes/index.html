Styles we install

Pick a pattern. We’ll handle the lines.

Most of the pattern is decided at the layout stage, before anything is stuck to the wall. That is where a backsplash either looks calm or looks busy.

Subway & stacked bond

The safe choice for a reason. Offsetting the joints softens a busy kitchen; stacked keeps it modern and horizontal.

Herringbone & chevron

More cuts, more waste, more time — and the pattern people notice from the doorway. Worth it in the right kitchen.

Mosaic & penny rounds

Sheet goods that follow the curve of a counter and hide the small irregularities of an older wall.

Large-format & slab

Fewer grout lines, a cleaner look. Needs a flat wall and a careful cut around outlets and the range hood.

Full-height to the cabinets

Tile run all the way up between counter and uppers, including around a window if you have one.

Grout that stays put

Grout chosen for a kitchen wall — not the floor grout, and not something that stains the first time sauce splashes.

Quoting a backsplash

Three photos and a tape measure gets you a price.

Backsplashes are usually the quickest job on the list. We will still give you a real window, not a shrug.

  • A wide photo of the kitchen wall, plus one close to the counter.
  • Rough length of the run, and the height you want tiled.
  • The tile you have chosen, or the size you are leaning towards.
  • Whether the old backsplash is still on the wall.

Buying tile yourself? Buy it before we start so the dye lot matches and nothing holds up the job.

The job

How a backsplash goes in.

Strip and flatten

Old tile off, wall made good. A backsplash is only as flat as the wall it sits on.

Set out, then set

Pattern dry-fitted and marked first, then tile set with full coverage and cuts around outlets and corners.

Grout, caulk, clean

Grout lines finished even, silicone where the tile meets the counter, counters wiped down before we go.

Questions

Backsplash questions, answered.

Do you remove the old backsplash first?

Yes. Old tile and adhesive come off, the wall is made good, and the new tile goes onto a flat surface. Tiling straight over old tile is a false economy and we do not do it.

Can you work around my cabinets and counters?

That is the normal job. The backsplash is fitted between the counter and the upper cabinets, with the cuts made to whatever is already there.

What happens with the outlets and switches?

The cover plates come off so the tile can be cut around the boxes accurately, then they go back on. Anything electrical beyond the covers is your electrician’s job, not ours.

How much tile should I buy?

Buy a little more than the math says. Once the run is measured we will tell you the number to order, including the waste for cuts and a couple of spares for later.
